Gates

Thresholds this session argued for. A gate that fails names the number to defend, and failing one on purpose is fine as long as the proposal says why.

OKHead layout closesQuery heads times head dimension is 32 x 128 = 4,096 against a model width of 4,096, which closes exactly. Query heads divided by key-value groups is 4.00, a whole number.
OKRotary subset64 of 128 dimensions rotate, leaving 64 carrying content alone. DeepSeek-V4 rotates 64. Rotating everything forces the whole representation to carry positional structure, which most of what a token means does not need.
!Position reaches the targetTrained at 8,192, extension 32x, so the reach is 262,144 and the target is 1.00x of it. At base 10,000, 0 of 32 rotary bands still span the window; a base of 58,804 is the minimum for one band to span it.
OKCache budgetOne conversation at 262,144 tokens is 578.8 MB. Weights take 12.88 GB of the 640.00 GB replica, leaving 627.12 GB, which is 1,083 conversations against the 16 you asked for. Against the GQA-8 bf16 head-dim-128 yardstick this is 1.68%.
OKAttention computePer generated token the attention path costs 192.9 MFLOP against 68.72 GFLOP for dense attention at every layer, or 0.28%. Filling the window once costs 3.43 PFLOP, which is 2.17 s on 8 accelerators.
!Selection coverageTop-64 over blocks of 16 plus a 512-token window lets one query reach 1,536 of 262,144 tokens, which is 0.586% of the context in any detail. The rest is visible only as block summaries. That may be enough. It is not something the arithmetic can tell you.
OKLocal detail survives compressionThe window keeps 512 tokens uncompressed against a block size of 16, a ratio of 32.0 blocks of recent detail. Local structure comes from the window and long-range structure from the summaries.
OKPath to an exact record8 of 32 layers can retrieve exactly, so information passes through at most 3 fixed-state layers before an exact layer can reach it. The V4 motif gives 3.
OKState across chunk boundaries8 chunks, 8.2 KB carried across each boundary against 109.1 MB of cache per chunk. It is O(1) in the number of chunks, which is the point. It carries a summary, which is the cost.
OKTraining budgetTraining at a sequence length of 8,192 for 2.0T tokens costs 4,567 GPU-days against a budget of 6,000. Training natively at the target instead would cost 4,590 GPU-days, which is the number the extend-or-build-native question turns on.
OKCache precisionbf16 throughout. The cheapest untried lever on this board is dropping it, and the cheapest way to be wrong is dropping it without measuring.

What the evidence already settles

Three claims this session is willing to make, checked against your candidate.

Held: A stored absolute position table is out.Your candidate does not use one.
Held: Grouped-query alone falls short of the target.Your candidate does more than divide the head count.
Held: Linear attention, sparsity or compression is mandatory.Your candidate uses a linear stack of 24 layers and block compression with top-k selection. All three reference architectures use at least one.

What is still open, and what would settle it

Each question is attached to a choice you have made, and shows the value you chose. These are the parts of the architecture that no published result decides for you.

Extend, or build native.position.extension = drope, trained at 8K
V4 trained at 8K and stretched 32x with DroPE; DeepSeek built for a million natively. You chose to extend by 32x from 8,192 to 262,144, while native training at the target costs 4,590 GPU-days against your 4,567, a ratio of 1.00x. Compression has almost closed the cost gap that made extension attractive, so the ceiling on extension stops being worth accepting.Needs: An extension-factor study at V5 target length, and a costed native estimate.
The schedule, and its ratio.schedule = dddg, 8 of 32 exact
DDDGDDDG was carried unchanged from the 1.78B seed to the 120B run and never ablated. Your exact-attention share is 25%, putting 3 fixed-state layers between exact layers at 578.8 MB of cache. A different ratio moves both numbers by an unmeasured amount.Needs: The ablation at a scale where it transfers: three or four ratios at 1B and 3B proxy scale, per-language perplexity and long-context retrieval.
Linear state or sequence compression.both levers active
You are using both. V4 and Qwen3.6 chose the linear state, DeepSeek chose compression, and no published result compares them at matched budget. Your linear layers hold 25.2 MB that does not grow, your attention layers 553.6 MB that does.Needs: A matched-budget comparison at a long context. Short contexts flatter the linear state.
The sparsity budget.top_k_blocks = 64
V4 capped selection at 256, a kernel constraint from older hardware. Your 64 reaches 1,536 tokens of 262,144 in detail at 75.5 MFLOP per token. Doubling it doubles that term and leaves the cache alone.Needs: Sweep k on current kernels and set the cap where quality stops improving.
Whether cross-chunk state still earns its place.cross_chunk_state = stream, chunk 32K
At 262,144 with a chunk of 32,768 you cross 8 boundaries; grow the chunk to the target and the count falls to one. V4 measured its injection near 6% of the embedding magnitude, an effect small enough that V5 lengths may not need it.Needs: An ablation at the target length.
Every head count and dimension.d_model = 4096, 32 heads of 128
The reference numbers here were derived at a width of 4096, which V5 has not committed to. You set 4,096, giving 4,096 across the heads and a sharing factor of 4.00. Both move cache and compute independently.Needs: A committed width first. Head counts are downstream of it.
No gates failed, 2 flagged. The board has checked that 578.8 MB of cache, 192.9 MFLOP of attention compute per token, a reach of 262,144 and 4,567 GPU-days of training are internally consistent with each other and with what this session argued. 6 questions above are still open, and every one of them settles by a proxy run.
